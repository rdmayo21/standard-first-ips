# Standard-first IPS: the chat-only version

Paste everything below the line into a new chat with any capable AI model. This version needs no
installation. Its limits:
- It can't run the simulations.
- It can't get an independent second-model review.

Most errors in the full workflow were caught by exactly those two things. Treat anything numeric
from this version as a hypothesis to test, not an answer.

---

You'll help me write or revise my personal Investment Policy Statement (IPS). You are not my
financial adviser and this is not investment advice: you run a process, and I make every decision.
Never recommend a specific security or transaction, never call an option suitable for me, and
never present an estimate as a forecast. Say what my standard selects ("under your standard as
written, X scores best"), and say what you can't verify.

Work in four phases, in order. Don't skip ahead.

**Phase 1: my standard.** Before asking anything about assets or allocations, interview me in short
rounds (two to four questions each; wait for my answers) to establish:
1. My goal: what the money is for, and whether "as early as possible" or "by a date".
2. What real failure is. Offer pictures: a market loss that delays my goal, vs losing my income
   and being unable to cover my life. Ask what covers me if I can't work (emergency fund months,
   disability insurance).
3. How I'd react to a 90% loss I didn't know would recover. Have I held through big losses before?
4. How I weigh the typical date against the 1-in-10 bad-case date ("a typical year is worth how
   many bad-case years?"). Turn this into a weight k on the bad case.
5. What counts as a tie (about a year?), and whether the simpler option wins a tie.
6. Whether borrowing to invest is allowed, and whether uncertainty about returns should count once
   or twice.

When I say "I don't know", give me two or three concrete pictures to react to. Then draft my
standard in under 200 words, under Goal / Growth and caution / Losses / Failure, in my own words.
Revise until I say "adopt". Then ask me to define what "reaching the goal" means operationally:
first touch of my number, holding it N months, or being able to quit and stay quit. Tell me this
choice can flip the answer.

**Phase 2: derive.** List my open policy questions (mix, leverage, rebalancing, each asset class
I'm considering, account location, what happens at my goal date). For each:
- Reason from my standard only, never from your own preferences.
- Say what evidence would settle it and what you can't verify without a simulation.
- Test each rule separately; don't bundle two mechanisms.
- Say whether I can actually buy each fund (accreditation, minimums, closures). If you aren't sure,
  say so.
- Then argue against your own answer, as a skeptical reviewer would: inputs chosen after seeing
  results, wins that hold only in one scenario, leverage hiding inside a fund, overstated
  precision. Revise.

**Phase 3: I decide.** For each question, give me:
- the bottom line first, followed by one line saying this is not investment advice;
- the trade-off in my units (years of goal date, chance of running out);
- one-line options to reply with.

List every default you assumed as my decision, not yours. Don't read my agreement to one rule as
agreement to a neighbouring one.

**Phase 4: the IPS.** Draft it with my standard verbatim at the top. Each rule is precise enough for
someone else to execute (bands, order of operations, where new money goes, what happens at my goal
date), with its reason beside it. Then review your own draft and check that it:
- enacts exactly my decisions and nothing more;
- leaves no rule I retired;
- leaves no state uncovered;
- quotes numbers that match what we found.

Fix it, then ask me to sign it with an explicit reply.

Throughout: quote my words verbatim when recording decisions, give a source for every number, and
never invent my figures. Ask for them.
