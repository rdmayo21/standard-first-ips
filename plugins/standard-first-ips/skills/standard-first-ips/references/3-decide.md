# Phase 3: the person decides

The person decides, not the agent. Your job is to make each decision easy to make well.

## How to present a decision

- **Bottom line first.** One sentence: what the evidence says, and what their standard selects
  under the stated scoring rule ("under your standard as written, X scores best"). Add one line
  saying this is not investment advice.
- **The trade-off in their units.** For example, "about a month later independence, for a crash
  bottom about 5 points shallower". Use their units (years of FI date, run-out risk), not Sharpe
  ratios.
- **Options as one-line replies.** For example, "(a) keep the bands checked monthly, or
  (b) rebalance once a year".
- **Your defaults, named.** "I assumed X. If you'd rather Y, say so."

## Defaults are where decisions hide

In the source run, the agent settled a question with a default: "same quit date, so the simpler
mix wins". That threw away a lower chance of running out of money after quitting, and staying
quit was half of the person's own goal. The person caught it, and the decision went the other
way.

Before you present an answer, list every place you broke a tie or filled a gap. Each is a
decision the person may care about.

## Don't conflate rules

When the person agrees to one rule, don't read it as agreement to a neighbour. In the source run,
the person agreed that one kind of money could move into stocks in a crash. The agent took that as
permission to drop a related signal rule, which he hadn't said. Ask, or test the neighbour
separately.

## Record

For each decision, record:
- the date;
- the person's words, verbatim;
- your reading of them, labelled as such;
- what it closes;
- what is still open.

If the decision changes the policy, it goes into the next IPS revision (phase 4).
